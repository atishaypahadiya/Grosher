Your list,

Grosher turns a grocery list into one route through the store, entrance to exit, and reroutes as you shop. It runs offline on the phone and resolves items with a model on my own laptop.

The app on the demo store

Demo Supercenter: a generated layout, not a real store yet.

Why

Stores are laid out to make you wander.

The milk is at the back so you pass everything else on the way. Retailer apps know where every item is shelved and will never route you out the shortest way. Grosher takes your list, finds each item's shelf, and gives you one route, entrance to exit, with the cold things last.

  • Shortest route, not list order

    The solver orders the stops. The order you typed is only the baseline it is measured against.

  • Offline in a store with no signal

    The app, the map and the catalog with its index stay on the phone after one visit.

  • Honest about guesses

    A model's answer shows as a guess, in amber, until you confirm the shelf.

  • Gets better every trip

    Got it and Not here move each item's confidence. A run of misses flags the aisle for review.

How it works

From a typed list to a walked route, and back again.

  1. 01

    Resolve the list

    Each line goes through six layers in order: an exact catalog item, your own memory of where you found it, an aisle sign that names it, a fuzzy match for typos, the local model, and finally a question for you. The catalog is 47,612 items built from the Instacart 2017 product data, mapped onto 298 aisle-sign categories and checked shelf by shelf by a reconcile pass.

  2. 02

    Solve the route

    The store is a graph of corridors in metres, with every shelf location a virtual node on its corridor, and Dijkstra gives the walk between any two. Up to 13 stops the order is exact, by Held–Karp; above that, 2-opt and Or-opt with delta evaluation. Cold last, heavy early and fragile late are penalties in metres, so they trade against distance instead of overriding it.

  3. 03

    Walk it

    The 3D map frames the current leg and lights the exact bay and shelf of the next item. Got it or Not here re-solves the remaining stops from the shelf you are standing at, and the camera eases to the new leg.

  4. 04

    Learn

    Every catalog row and memory carries a confidence in three bands. Got it moves it 30% of the way to certain, Not here takes 0.3 off, and anything unconfirmed for 90 days decays. Three misses in one aisle, or a quarter of a trip, flag the store as possibly reset.

Two rules
  1. 1
    A guess is always shown as a guess.A model's answer is drawn in amber as "probably …" and flagged on the trip until you confirm it.
  2. 2
    Your confirmation beats the catalog.A shelf you confirmed wins over the catalog's for that item; a generated catalog can be wrong.

The map's visual language
  • WalkedWhite at 40%, solid: the route behind you.
  • Current legWhite at full strength with a soft glow: where you walk now.
  • AheadDashed: the rest of the route, re-solved at every check-off.
  • YouThe lime dot with a slow halo. Lime only ever means now.
  • Lit slotThe exact bay and shelf of the next item, seen through the shelving.
  • DoneA white ring with a check where an item was collected.
Limits

What it does not do yet.

  • A generated layoutThe Demo Supercenter is generated; real aisles replace it after the Story Rd survey.
  • The resolver needs my laptop awakeThe model runs on my Mac behind a tunnel and a token gate. When it sleeps, unknown items wait for a tap; everything the catalog and memory can place still works.
  • iOS standalone voice is unverifiedHold-to-speak is hidden in home-screen mode until it is tested on a device; keyboard dictation covers it.
  • Tap to advance, no live dotPosition moves when you tap Got it or Not here; there is no indoor positioning yet.
  • One storeThe app knows one map today.
